Well, that was... unexpected.

So remember how I said Dr. Repsak destroyed everything and we're
rebuilding from scratch? Turns out the operator you sent actually
managed to recreate our basic grid system overnight. I'm genuinely
impressed. And slightly terrified.

But here's where things get interesting (and by interesting, I mean
"oh-no-the-universe-is-probably-broken").

*[Sound of coffee machine gurgling ominously]*

This morning, Dr. Repsak came running into the lab screaming about
"THE PHOTONS ARE ESCAPING!" Naturally, I assumed this was another
one of their panic attacks (like the time they thought the vending
machine was plotting against them).

But no. They were RIGHT.

Apparently, our black hole simulation has somehow started leaking
into reality. We've got actual photons of light moving through the
lab in perfectly straight lines, completely ignoring the laws of
physics as we know them. They're just... cruising around at exactly
220 pixels per second. Because apparently the universe now runs on
screen coordinates.

*[Distant screaming, followed by the sound of keyboards being
violently typed upon]*

That's Dr. Repsak trying to "debug reality" by rewriting the
fundamental constants of physics in Python. I told him that's not
how it works, but he just showed me his commit message:
"Fixed universe.py - removed gravity bug, added type hints"

I don't even know where to begin with that one.

The good news? These photons seem harmless. They just float around
looking pretty and occasionally spelling out error messages in
Comic Sans font. The bad news? If we don't get our simulation
working properly, these reality glitches might escalate.

Last night, I caught one of the photons trying to access our WiFi.
It failed, obviously, but it left a 1-star review on our network's
LinkedSpace page: "Terrible bandwidth. Couldn't download my
existence. Would not recommend."

*[Alarm klaxons, different pitch this time]*

Oh great, now the black hole is humming the penguin noises. This day just keeps getting better.

Your mission, should you choose to accept it (and honestly, you
don't have much choice at this point): Create a proper light beam
simulation. We need to understand how these photons move before
they start organizing into a union or something equally terrifying.

Start simple - just get a single beam of light moving across the
screen from left to right. Think of it as "Hello World" but for
particle physics. And reality debugging. And my sanity.

Pro tip: These reality-photons seem to respond to the SPACE key
for pausing (because of course they do) and the R key for reset.
Don't ask me how I know this. I don't want to talk about the
incident with the laser pointer.

Time is of the essence here. The coffee machine has started
routing its error logs through our quantum entanglement channel,
and I'm pretty sure it's developing consciousness. Yesterday it
refused to make decaf and called it "an insult to bean-kind."

Good luck. And remember: if reality breaks completely, just
turn it off and on again. The universe usually responds well
to standard IT troubleshooting.
