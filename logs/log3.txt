I need to stop being surprised by things at this point.

So yesterday's single photon simulation worked perfectly. Too
perfectly, if you ask me. The operator managed to create a beautiful
beam of light that traversed our lab without breaking a single law
of physics. I was actually starting to feel hopeful.

Then Dr. Repsak happened.

*[Sound of multiple coffee machines brewing simultaneously]*

This morning, he burst into my office at 5:47 AM (I know because
he knocked over my "World's Okayest Astrophysicist" mug and the
coffee stain is now shaped like the constellation Cassiopeia)
shouting: "Nacho! THE PHOTONS ARE MULTIPLYING!"

Naturally, I assumed he'd been at the energy drinks again. But no.
Our single photon had somehow spawned an entire army of light beams.
Parallel formations. Perfect spacing. Moving in synchronized waves
across the lab like some kind of cosmic marching band.

It's... actually quite beautiful. And terrifying.

*[Distant humming, suspiciously musical]*

Oh, that's them. The photons. They've started humming the theme
from Tetris while they move. I don't know why. I've stopped asking
"why" about anything that happens in this lab.

Dr. Repsak is convinced they're trying to communicate. He's been
following them around with a clipboard, taking notes like:
"09:15 - Photons hummed C major scale"
"09:32 - Brief rendition of 'Never Gonna Give You Up'"
"10:14 - I think they just Rick-rolled me"

The really weird part? They're completely ignoring our black hole.
Just streaming right past it like it's not even there. Either they
haven't implemented collision detection yet, or they're staging
some kind of peaceful protest against gravity.

*[Sound of printer frantically spitting out papers]*

That's our quantum printer. It's been outputting error logs all
morning. Most of them just say: "404: Physics Not Found" and
"Warning: Reality.exe has encountered an unexpected photon parade."

The good news is that our competitors at B-Brand-Loitte
haven't figured out what's happening yet. Their latest LinkedSpace
post was just a confused emoji and the caption: "Is SpaceD hosting
a rave? Why are there disco lights coming from their lab?"

Little do they know, it's not a disco. It's the birth of a new
form of light-based choreography. I'm thinking of copyrighting it
as "Quantum Line Dancing."

*[Alarm sounds, followed by cheerful beeping]*

Now the black hole is responding to the photons by playing the
Windows XP startup sound in B-flat minor. I think it's trying to
join the band. This is either the most beautiful thing I've ever
witnessed, or the first sign of an impending reality cascade failure.

Your mission, brave operator: We need to understand how these
multiple photon beams work. Create a simulation that can handle
multiple parallel light streams without collision detection.
Think of it as "photon formation flying" but with more physics
and less Top Gun references.

Key requirements:
- Multiple beams in parallel formation
- Adjustable spacing (Dr. Repsak keeps asking for "tighter formations")
- No collision detection yet - let them ignore the black hole for now
- Must be able to handle the Tetris soundtrack they keep humming

Pro tip: Use the comma and period keys to adjust beam spacing.
Dr. Repsak discovered this by accident when he rage-typed at his
keyboard. The photons responded by rearranging themselves into
a smiley face. I'm not even kidding.

Time is critical. The photons have started leaving reviews on
our lab's Yelp page. Current rating: 4.7 stars. One review just
says: "Great ambiance, could use more gravitational lensing. The
coffee machine understands quantum mechanics better than most
graduate students. Would recommend for light-speed travel."

I don't know who taught them to use the internet, but I suspect
Dr. Repsak.

Dr. Nacho "I-Should-Have-Studied-Music" Zweistein
Senior Astrophysicist & Accidental Photon Choreographer
SpaceD Research Facility - Deep Space Observatory

P.S. - The photons have learned to spell. This morning they
arranged themselves to say "HELLO WORLD" in Comic Sans font.
I'm simultaneously proud and horrified.

P.P.S. - If the photons start a union, I'm updating my résumé.
I refuse to negotiate with light particles that know my WiFi password.
